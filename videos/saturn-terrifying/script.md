[VISUAL: title card — "SATURN" in gold on black, low hum.]
[SLOW] This is why Saturn might be the most terrifying planet of them all. [PAUSE]

### BEAT 1 — THE HEXAGON — ~0:35
[VISUAL: top-down on the north pole, the six-sided storm sharp against the clouds.]
[SERIOUS] Start with the thing that should not exist. At Saturn's north pole sits a storm shaped like a near-perfect hexagon.

[VISUAL: a faint geometric overlay tracing the six straight sides.]
Six straight sides. Six sharp corners. Nature does not usually draw straight lines this big.

[VISUAL: a small Earth dropped beside the hexagon for scale — Earth is dwarfed.]
[EMPHASIS] And it is enormous. Each side is wider than the entire planet Earth. You could drop four Earths inside it.

[VISUAL: zoom into the spinning vortex at the hexagon's center.]
At its center spins a hurricane fifty times larger than the biggest hurricane on Earth. It has been turning there for as long as we have been able to look.

[VISUAL: the hexagon glowing, slow rotation, cold blue light.]
[LOW] No one is completely sure why it holds that shape. A storm the size of a continent, locked into geometry, on a planet where nothing should be solid at all.

### BEAT 2 — THE WINDS — ~1:30
[VISUAL: cloud bands streaking past at blurring speed.]
[TENSION] Because Saturn is not solid. It is a giant ball of gas and liquid, and it is moving.

[VISUAL: wind-speed readout climbing — 500, 1000, 1500 km/h.]
[EMPHASIS] The winds near its equator reach around eighteen hundred kilometers an hour. More than a thousand miles per hour.

[VISUAL: a passenger jet swallowed and shredded by the gale.]
That is faster than the speed of sound. Fast enough to tear apart anything we could ever build and throw the pieces around the planet.

[VISUAL: endless churning cloud ocean to the horizon, no land anywhere.]
[LOW] And there is nowhere to hide from it. No mountains. No ground. Just thousands of kilometers of screaming wind, all the way down.

### BEAT 3 — NOWHERE TO LAND — ~2:20
[VISUAL: a falling POV plunging through cloud layer after cloud layer.]
[SERIOUS] So what happens if you tried to land on Saturn? You couldn't. There is no surface to land on.

[VISUAL: layers darkening from gold to orange to deep red as we fall.]
You would fall through the clouds, into thicker and thicker gas, as it gets darker, hotter, and heavier around you.

[VISUAL: the pressure crushing a metal sphere flat.]
[DARK] Long before you ever reached anything solid, the pressure would crush you, and the heat would vaporize you. Saturn does not have a shore. It has a throat.

[VISUAL: faint glow rising from the deep below.]
[QUIET] And the deeper you go, the stranger it gets.

### BEAT 4 — DIAMOND RAIN — ~3:10
[VISUAL: lightning flashing inside deep Saturn clouds.]
[TENSION] In the upper atmosphere, gigantic lightning storms rip through clouds of methane.

[VISUAL: methane molecules breaking, soot forming and drifting down.]

[VISUAL: the falling carbon compressing, hardening, beginning to glint.]
[EMPHASIS] As it falls, the crushing pressure squeezes that carbon harder and harder. First into graphite. Then into something else.

[VISUAL: glittering diamonds raining down through the dark.]
[SLOW] Diamonds. Scientists think it may literally rain diamonds inside Saturn. A storm of jewels, falling forever into a place no one will ever see.

### BEAT 5 — THE RINGS ARE DYING — ~4:05
[VISUAL: pull back out to the magnificent rings, backlit by the sun.]
[CALM] Then there are the rings. The most beautiful thing in the solar system.

[VISUAL: close on the rings resolving into countless tumbling chunks of ice.]

[VISUAL: the ring particles slowly drifting inward, streaking down toward Saturn.]
[TENSION] And here is the unsettling part. The rings are falling. Saturn's gravity is slowly pulling them in. A constant rain of ice pouring down onto the planet.

[VISUAL: timelapse — the rings thinning, fading, gone.]
[HEAVY] In maybe a hundred million years, the rings could be gone completely. We may be living in the one brief moment in history when Saturn has rings at all. [PAUSE]

### BEAT 6 — IT GLOWS FROM WITHIN — ~5:05
[VISUAL: Saturn in darkness, a deep internal heat glowing through the clouds.]
[SERIOUS] Saturn hides another secret. It glows. It gives off more heat than it receives from the distant sun.

[VISUAL: deep interior — droplets of helium forming and falling like rain.]
[LOW] Deep inside, under unimaginable pressure, helium turns to liquid and rains down toward the core. That endless rain releases heat, and the whole planet burns from the inside.

[VISUAL: the crushing core, faintly glowing, surrounded by black metallic liquid.]
[DARK] At the center is a core wrapped in liquid metal hydrogen, under pressures we cannot recreate on Earth. A furnace, sealed beneath a thousand kilometers of storm.

### BEAT 7 — THE GREAT WHITE STORM — ~6:00
[VISUAL: a small bright spot appearing in Saturn's clouds.]
[STEADY] Every Saturn year, roughly every thirty Earth years, something wakes up.

[VISUAL: the spot exploding outward into a vast white storm wrapping the planet.]
[TENSION] A monstrous storm called the Great White Spot erupts and grows until it wraps the entire planet. A band of chaos tens of thousands of kilometers long.

[VISUAL: lightning crawling across the giant storm front.]
[EMPHASIS] One of these storms grew so large its own tail caught up with its head and collided with it. A storm eating itself, on a world the size of nine Earths.

[VISUAL: a lone spacecraft, Cassini, drifting near the rings.]

[VISUAL: the craft threading the narrow gap between the planet and the rings.]

[VISUAL: the craft burning up, tumbling, signal fading into the clouds.]

[VISUAL: Enceladus — a white moon firing geysers of ice into space.]
