=== CHUNK 1 — COLD OPEN ===
Right now you think you are awake, alert, and seeing the room around you exactly as it is. You are wrong on all three. You are half-blind, a beat behind, and watching a recording your brain made a fraction of a second ago. And to keep that recording smooth, your brain cuts things out. Quietly. Constantly. Without telling you. Most of the time, you never notice the cut. But every so often, the edit slips, and for one second you see the seam. Scientists have names for these moments. Each one is a glitch in the machine that builds your reality. Six of them. By the end, you will not be sure how much of today you actually saw. Let's start with the smallest one.

=== CHUNK 2 — DOORWAY EFFECT ===
You stand up to get something. A clear, simple reason. You walk to the next room, and the moment you cross the doorway, it is gone. Why did I come in here. You stand there, empty. This is the Doorway Effect, and it is not a memory problem. Your brain files memories by room. Each space is a folder. Walk through a doorway, and your brain closes the old folder to open a new one. The thought you were holding was inside the folder it just shut. The doorway is not where you forgot. It is where your brain decided to forget for you. To get it back, you walk back. Standing in the old room reopens the folder. A tiny edit you make dozens of times a day, and almost never catch. Harmless. Now they start getting stranger.

=== CHUNK 3 — SPOTLIGHT EFFECT ===
You walk into a room and trip on nothing. Instantly, you are certain everyone saw it. Everyone is still thinking about it. They are not. Almost nobody noticed. And the few who did forgot in seconds. This is the Spotlight Effect. You overestimate how much others see you, by a lot. The reason is simple and a little sad. You are the main character of your own life. You live every second from inside your own head, so it feels like the center of every room. But everyone else is the main character of theirs. Their spotlight is on them, not you. The coffee stain you hid all day. The sentence you replayed for an hour. You were the only person in the world running that footage. The spotlight you feel is real. It is just that you are holding it. And pointing it at yourself. Which raises a question. If your brain invents an audience, what else does it invent? As it turns out, it can invent your taste.

=== CHUNK 4 — MERE EXPOSURE EFFECT ===
Think of a song you hated the first time you heard it. And somehow now you like it. You did not decide to like it. It just got familiar. And familiar feels like safe. This is the Mere Exposure Effect. The more your brain sees a thing, the more it trusts it. To a brain built for survival, new means risk. Familiar means it did not kill you last time. So repetition quietly rewrites preference into comfort. You start to like what you simply see often. And an entire industry runs on this seam. The ad you scroll past and think you ignored. You did not ignore it. You filed it. The brand you reach for without thinking is often just the one you have seen the most. You think you chose it. Exposure chose it for you, one glance at a time. Your taste can be installed. And the next glitch goes after your body.

=== CHUNK 5 — HYPNIC JERK ===
You are almost asleep. Calm. Letting go. And your whole body kicks, like you missed a step on the stairs. You jolt awake, heart pounding, sure for half a second that you were falling. Nothing touched you. You did not move on purpose. So what threw you? Falling asleep is not a smooth slide. It is a handoff between two systems. Sometimes your muscles go limp before your alert system has fully powered down. And an ancient alarm, older than humans, misreads the sudden stillness as falling out of a tree. So it fires one hard signal to every muscle. Wake up. Grab something. The falling dream you remember came after the twitch, not before. Your brain wrote it in a fraction of a second to explain the jolt. A story stitched over a glitch, so fast you remember the story as the cause. We broke this one down in full on the channel. Next: a glitch that hijacks you for days.

=== CHUNK 6 — EARWORM ===
A song starts playing in your head right now. You did not choose it. You cannot switch it off. It plays the same five seconds, over and over, chasing an ending it never reaches. This is an earworm, and it runs on a glitch from 1927. A researcher named Zeigarnik found your brain cannot stand anything left unfinished. A finished task, it drops. An interrupted one, it keeps poking you to complete. A chorus is the perfect trap. Catchy, repetitive, and almost never heard all the way to the end. So your brain files it as unfinished business and replays the loop, trying to close it. The more you fight it, the more you rehearse it. Attention is the food it eats. The way out is the opposite. Let the song finish in your head, or chew gum to jam the loop. An idle mind is an open door, and the catchiest thing nearby walks straight in. Now the last one. This is where reality itself slips.

=== CHUNK 7 — DEJA VU ===
You walk into a place you have never been. And you know it. You have stood here before. Said this before. The feeling is total, and impossible. This is deja vu, and the leading idea is unsettling. It is a filing error. A moment is supposed to go through short-term experience first, then into memory. In deja vu, the present slips straight into the memory drawer before you finish living it. So the moment arrives already stamped: old. You are remembering something as it happens. Your brain double-exposed a single second of your life. Lived and remembered at the same time. For that second, you catch your own mind in the act of building the past. It is the closest you will ever get to watching reality render in real time. Six glitches. Now step back and look at what they have in common.

=== CHUNK 8 — FINALE ===
Now here is where almost everyone gets it wrong. Most people hear all this and think their brain is glitching. Broken. Unreliable. Something to fix. The truth is the exact opposite. None of these are your brain failing. Every one is your brain working exactly as designed. It forgets at doorways to stay fast. It invents an audience to keep you in line. It installs your taste, rewrites your sleep, loops your attention, and back-dates your memories. And here is the part almost nobody says out loud. Your brain had a choice to make millions of years ago. Be slow and accurate, or be fast and good enough. A slow, perfectly accurate brain gets you killed. So evolution chose speed, and paid for it with these tiny errors. The glitches are not the price of a broken brain. They are the price of a brain fast enough to keep you alive. You never see reality directly. You see your brain's best guess about it, a beat late, heavily edited. A controlled hallucination, tuned just well enough to survive. The glitches are not the system breaking. They are the only moments you ever get to see the system at all. So the real question is not why your brain glitches. It is how much of today you actually saw, and how much your brain quietly filled in for you. Which of these six have you felt? And which one just happened to you while watching this? Tell me below.
